Eighteen small problems. Write an expression in terms of x that solves each one, and the engine judges it as you type — against the examples you can see and against tests you cannot. The score is the number of characters. Most of these are two or three.

How the judging works

Your expression becomes a one-argument function and is run against every test by the engine itself. The comparison is ~, match, not = — so 1 2 3 and 1 2 3.0 are not the same answer, and something of the wrong shape cannot slip through by conforming.

A half-written expression is judged rather than thrown, because golf is typed one character at a time and most of what the judge sees is not valid yet. An error just means no test passed.

Par is a known short solution, not a proven minimum. If you get under it, you have found something better — and the share link carries your expression so you can prove it.
